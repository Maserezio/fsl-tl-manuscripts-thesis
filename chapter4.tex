\chapter{Experiments and Results}
\label{ch:data}\label{ch:results}

This chapter describes the data and the setup of the experiments and presents the results in the order of the \glspl{rq}. Section~\ref{sec:datasets} introduces the eleven datasets and their roles, and Section~\ref{metrics} defines the evaluation metrics. The training settings of the pipelines are given in Appendix~\ref{app:training-settings}. Sections~\ref{sec:results-models} and~\ref{sec:results-reconstruction} answer \gls{rq}~1 with the encoders, initializations, and line reconstructions pipelines. Section~\ref{sec:results-rq2} reduces the number of training pages for \gls{rq}~2, and Section~\ref{sec:results-rq3} evaluates the transfer to other collections for \gls{rq}~3. All metrics are given in percent, and higher values are better.


\section{Datasets}
\label{sec:datasets}\label{sec:data-benchmarks}\label{sec:data-pretraining}\label{sec:data-cross}

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:dataset-splits}, the experiments use eleven historical document datasets. DIVA-HisDB and U-DIADS-TL are used to compare the pipelines, encoders, and initializations and to study the number of training pages. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:gt-formats}, the two benchmarks use different \gls{gt} formats, binary line masks and line polygons  respectively, so every  \gls{tls} pipeline produces both. DIVA-HisDB~\cite{diva_hisdb} contains main text, glosses, decorations, and overlapping lines, and the experiments use its Task~2 PAGE XML annotations. Two larger collections are used for the initializations. CATMuS Medieval~\cite{catmus-medieval} is used for supervised pretraining on its line polygons. Its training split contains 15 pages of the three DIVA-HisDB manuscripts with the same line annotations, 14 of them DIVA-HisDB test pages. These 15 pages with 1,269 lines are removed. CATMuS Medieval contains none of the U-DIADS-TL manuscripts. cBAD 2019~\cite{cbad-2019} is used without labels for knowledge distillation~\cite{beyer2022knowledgedistillationgoodteacher} with DINOv3 as the teacher model. The remaining seven collections form the pool for the cross-collection experiments. They cover five scripts, Hebrew, Syriac, Arabic, Greek, and Latin, as well as different line structures, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:rq3-collections}.

\section{Evaluation Metrics}
\label{metrics}

Evaluation uses five metrics computed with the DIVA Line Segmentation Evaluator~\cite{diva_tool} and FEST evaluator for U-DIADS-TL~\cite{Zottin_2025_FEST}:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \Gls{pixeliu} measures the overlap between predicted and ground-truth foreground pixels:
    \[\text{Pixel IU} = \frac{TP}{TP + FP + FN}.\]

    \item \Gls{lineiu} applies the same intersection-over-union principle at the text-line level:
    \[\text{Line IU} = \frac{TP}{TP + FP + FN}.\]
    
    \item \Gls{dr} measures the proportion of ground-truth lines that are matched:
    \[\text{DR} = \frac{M}{N_1},\]
    where $M$ is the number of matched lines and $N_1$ is the number of ground-truth lines.

    \item \Gls{ra} measures the proportion of predicted lines that are matched:
    \[\text{RA} = \frac{M}{N_2},\]
    where $N_2$ is the number of predicted lines.

    \item \Gls{fm} is the harmonic mean of DR and RA:
    \[\text{FM} = \frac{2\cdot\text{DR}\cdot\text{RA}}{\text{DR}+\text{RA}}.\]
\end{enumerate}

\section{Encoders, Initializations, and Line Reconstruction}
\label{sec:results-models}

Of the stages in Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline-overview}, this section varies the encoder, its initialization, and the line reconstruction. It compares every combination of pipeline, encoder, and initialization on the complete benchmark partitions. On U-DIADS-TL, these are three training pages per manuscript, so that \gls{rq}~1 is a few-shot setting on U-DIADS-TL. On DIVA-HisDB, these are all 20 training pages per manuscript. All results are reported as the line-level \gls{fm} of Section~\ref{metrics}. The center-line model uses ConvNeXt-Tiny with CATMuS initialization only. It takes part in the comparison with the published results and in the line reconstruction, but not in the comparison of encoders and initializations. The best configurations are first compared with the published results. Then, the encoder and the initialization are compared as separate factors, and finally, the predictions of a pipeline are kept fixed and the step that turns them into lines is varied.

\subsection{Encoders and Initializations}
\label{sec:results-rq1-summary}

Table~\ref{tab:rq1-paired} compares results that differ in one factor only, that is, results with the same pipeline, benchmark, and other factor. The encoder family has no consistent effect. Compared with ConvNeXt-Tiny, PVTv2-B2 is better in 10 of 20 pairs and ViT-Small in 10 of 26 pairs, with median differences of $-0.03$ and $-0.76$ percentage points. The initialization has a larger effect. CATMuS is better than ImageNet in 11 of 12 pairs, with a median difference of 1.42 percentage points, and random weights are worse than ImageNet in 16 of 18 pairs. The two DINOv3 variants are better than ImageNet in only 6 and 4 of 12 pairs. The largest effect occurs for RT-DETR + BBox U-Net, where CATMuS improves the U-DIADS-TL result by 6.04--20.76 percentage points over ImageNet with the same encoder. The encoder size has a small effect as well. As shown in Appendix~\ref{app:rq1-size}, larger encoders improve the U-DIADS-TL result of Mask R-CNN and the U-Net with projection cuts by only 1.5 and 1.6 percentage points. The pooled means per factor are given in Appendix~\ref{app:rq1-subsets}.

This effect differs between the pipelines. Trained from random weights, RT-DETR + BBox U-Net reaches only 33.39\% on U-DIADS-TL, while all three of its encoders reach 89.90--91.71\% after CATMuS pretraining. Mask R-CNN and the U-Net with projection cuts depend less on the starting weights. However, random weights also give their lowest U-DIADS-TL results, with 78.00\% and 79.89\% for ConvNeXt-Tiny. From random weights, RT-DETR + BBox U-Net segments most lines only in parts or not at all. From ImageNet weights, it misses individual lines, and from CATMuS weights, it segments almost all of them.

\section{Cross-Collection Robustness}
\label{sec:results-rq3}

The following experiments assess how robust a few-shot model is on manuscript collections that were not seen during training. They follow two ideas. First, the number of source collections changes step by step, from no page of the target collection and a single source collection to all other collections in the \gls{loco} setting. The continuous hold-out connects the single-collection and the \gls{loco} setting, as it removes the source collections one after another. Second, a variation of \gls{loco}-1 keeps six training pages but takes them from six, three, or two source collections. Of the stages in Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline-overview}, this section keeps the pipelines fixed and varies the source collections of the training pages. Mask R-CNN, the best pipeline of \gls{rq}~1 and \gls{rq}~2, is compared with the center-line model from the semantic paradigm, to test whether its advantage holds on unseen collections.

The transfer settings and the models are described in Section~\ref{sec:meth-transfer}, and the seven cross-collection datasets in Section~\ref{sec:datasets}. Most of these datasets have no official partition. Therefore, each of them is partitioned once into three training pages, 10\% of the pages for validation, and the remaining pages for testing. Pinkas and NorHand v3 keep their official test sets and take their training and validation pages from the official training part, which for NorHand v3 is a random subset of 30 pages.

The continuous hold-out connects the single-collection and the \gls{loco} setting. It starts from \gls{loco}-3 and removes one source collection after the other until a single source collection remains. GRPOLY-DB lies apart from the other collections and is held out first, and NorHand v3 and RASAM are its nearest collections, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:rq3-collection-pc1}. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:rq3-stepdown}, the transfer of Mask R-CNN to GRPOLY-DB depends on which collections remain among the sources. As long as NorHand v3 and RASAM remain, it reaches 95.89--99.59\%. Without them, its result drops to 31.21\%, and with only Pinkas and Phil.\ gr.\ 130 to 0.81\%.
