\section{Datasets}
\label{sec:datasets}
There are eleven historical-document datasets used for experiments , summarized in Table~\ref{tab:dataset-splits}. U-DIADS-TL and DIVA-HisDB are the target benchmarks for the backbone comparison and the shot-selection experiments. CATMuS Medieval is used for supervised pretraining and cBAD 2019 for unsupervised knowledge distillation~\cite{beyer2022knowledgedistillationgoodteacher} with DINOv3 as the teacher model. The remaining seven collections form the cross-collection pool for the few-shot robustness experiments.
Table~\ref{tab:dataset-splits} gives the number of training, validation, and test pages of each dataset; for U-DIADS-TL and DIVA-HisDB, the counts refer to each manuscript, ONB Cod.\ Syr.\ 1, RASAM, RASM2018/2019, Phil.\ gr.\ 130, and GRPOLY-DB are released without an official partition. To give all seven cross-collection datasets the same conditions, each of them is split into three training pages, a validation set, and a test set, whereby Pinkas and NorHand v3 keep their official test sets;

\paragraph{U-DIADS-TL:} U-DIADS-TL~\cite{Zottin_2025_FEST} is the benchmark of the ICDAR 2025 FEST competition. It contains 28 pages from each of the manuscripts Latin2, Latin14396, and Syriaque 341, annotated with binary pixel-level line masks. The validation pages are used only for model and postprocessing selection.
\paragraph{DIVA-HisDB:} DIVA-HisDB~\cite{diva_hisdb} contains 50 pages from each of Codex Bodmer 55 (CB55), Codex Sangallensis 18 (CSG18), and Codex Sangallensis 863 (CSG863), with main text, glosses, decorations, and overlapping lines. The experiments use the Task~2 PAGE XML annotations, which provide a polygon and a baseline for each text line.
\paragraph{CATMuS Medieval:} CATMuS Medieval~\cite{catmus-medieval} contains medieval Latin-script manuscripts in Latin, French, Iberian, and Italian with PAGE XML line polygons, which are used for supervised pretraining.
\paragraph{cBAD 2019:} cBAD 2019~\cite{cbad-2019} contains pages from seven European archives, annotated with text regions and baselines but without line polygons. Only its page images are used, without labels, for representation distillation.
\paragraph{Pinkas:} Pinkas~\cite{Barakat2019Pinkas} contains 30 pages of early-modern records of the Frankfurt Jewish community in Hebrew, with bleed-through, stains, faded ink, and overlapping lines. Three training pages without valid line polygons are excluded.
\paragraph{ONB Cod.\ Syr.\ 1:} ONB Cod.\ Syr.\ 1~\cite{AboudIshac2025ONBSyr1} contains 140 folios of a 16th-century Syriac manuscript of the Austrian National Library with one main-text column; short, partly vertical marginal lines are annotated as separate lines.
\paragraph{RASAM:} RASAM~\cite{VidalGorene2021RASAM} contains 517 pages from 18 Arabic manuscripts in Maghrebi scripts of the BULAC library, combining both released parts. The images are taken from the library's IIIF server, and the polygons are rescaled where the image resolution differs from the PAGE XML page size.
\paragraph{RASM2018/2019:} RASM2018/2019~\cite{KeinanSchoonbaert2019RASM} contains 120 pages of Arabic scientific manuscripts from the 10th to the 19th century from the British Library, with 2,620 line polygons. Its pages measure up to \(6464\times8984\) pixels, the highest resolution in the pool.
\paragraph{Phil.\ gr.\ 130:} Phil.\ gr.\ 130~\cite{SindlevAndersen2026PhilGrGT,SindlevAndersen2026MedievalGreekHTR} contains 18 pages of a 14th-century Greek manuscript of the Austrian National Library with 847 line polygons; the images are not part of the release and are taken from the library viewer. With about 47 lines per page, it is the densest collection in the pool.
\paragraph{GRPOLY-DB:} Of GRPOLY-DB~\cite{Gatos2015GRPOLY}, only the handwritten part is used: 46 pages of Greek polytonic documents with 693 line polygons. The pages contain few, widely spaced lines, and the polygons enclose a wide band around the handwriting.
\paragraph{NorHand v3:} NorHand v3~\cite{Beyer2023NorHand} contains Norwegian letters and diaries from the 19th and early 20th centuries. The official test set is kept, and a random subset of 30 official training pages, drawn with seed 42, serves as the pool for training and validation pages. The line polygons range from narrow strips to irregular contours.

\section{Evaluation Metrics}
\label{metrics}

Evaluation uses five metrics computed with the DIVA Line Segmentation Evaluator~\cite{diva_tool}: Pixel Intersection over Union (Pixel IU), Line Intersection over Union (Line IU), Detection Rate (DR), Recognition Accuracy (RA), and F-Measure (FM). For U-DIADS-TL, the FEST evaluator is used instead~\cite{Zottin_2025_FEST}.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \Gls{pixeliu} measures the overlap between predicted and ground-truth foreground pixels:
    \[\text{Pixel IU} = \frac{TP}{TP + FP + FN}.\]

    \item \Gls{lineiu} applies the same intersection-over-union principle at the text-line level:
    \[\text{Line IU} = \frac{TP}{TP + FP + FN}.\]
    Here, $TP$, $FP$, and $FN$ denote correctly detected, extra, and missed lines, respectively. A prediction is correct when both its pixel precision and recall are at least $75\%$.

    \item \Gls{dr} measures the proportion of ground-truth lines that are matched:
    \[\text{DR} = \frac{M}{N_1},\]
    where $M$ is the number of matched lines and $N_1$ is the number of ground-truth lines.

    \item \Gls{ra} measures the proportion of predicted lines that are matched:
    \[\text{RA} = \frac{M}{N_2},\]
    where $N_2$ is the number of predicted lines.

    \item \Gls{fm} is the harmonic mean of DR and RA:
    \[\text{FM} = \frac{2\cdot\text{DR}\cdot\text{RA}}{\text{DR}+\text{RA}}.\]
\end{enumerate}

\section{Backbones}
\label{sec:backbones}

All segmentation approaches of Section~\ref{sec:tls-methods} are evaluated with the same encoder backbones, which are combined with several initializations.

\subsection{Encoder Architectures}
\label{sec:encoder-architectures}

Three encoder families are compared: ConvNeXt~\cite{liu2022convnet2020s} as a modern convolutional network, the Pyramid Vision Transformer v2 (PVTv2) as a hierarchical transformer, and the plain \gls{vit}~\cite{dosovitskiy2021imageworth16x16words}. ConvNeXt and PVTv2 produce feature maps at four resolutions with strides of 4 to 32 pixels. A \gls{vit} produces a single feature map with a stride of 16 pixels, which is transformed into a feature pyramid: transposed convolutions upsample it to strides of 4 and 8 pixels, and max-pooling downsamples it to a stride of 32 pixels.

\subsection{Initializations}
\label{sec:initializations}

Five initialization strategies are considered. They determine the encoder's starting weights, except for CATMuS pretraining, which initializes the complete model.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Random.} The encoder weights are initialized randomly.
    \item \textbf{ImageNet.} The encoder uses weights obtained by supervised image-classification pretraining on ImageNet~\cite{deng2009imagenet,russakovsky2015imagenet}.
    \item \textbf{DINOv3 (LVD-1689M).} The encoder uses weights obtained by self-supervised DINOv3 pretraining on LVD-1689M~\cite{dinov3}.
    \item \textbf{DINOv3 (cBAD).} Knowledge distillation~\cite{beyer2022knowledgedistillationgoodteacher} transfers representations from a frozen DINOv3 ViT-B/16 teacher pretrained on LVD-1689M~\cite{dinov3} to the encoder using unlabeled cBAD 2019 pages~\cite{cbad-2019}. Training uses LightlyTrain~0.15.0 DistillationV3 (Table~\ref{tab:distillation}).
    \item \textbf{CATMuS.} The complete model is pretrained with supervision on CATMuS Medieval line polygons. The resulting encoder and task-head weights initialize few-shot fine-tuning (Table~\ref{tab:catmus-pretraining}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Text Line Segmentation Methods}
\label{sec:tls-methods}

Three segmentation approaches are evaluated: semantic segmentation, two-stage detection and segmentation, and instance segmentation. All three use the encoders of Section~\ref{sec:backbones} and handle both ground-truth formats, binary line masks and PAGE XML polygons.

\subsection{Semantic Segmentation}
\label{sec:semantic-postprocessing}

The semantic segmentation model predicts a single text-line probability map, which postprocessing splits into line instances (Figure~\ref{fig:semantic-postprocessing} and Algorithm~\ref{alg:semantic-postprocessing}). The map is binarized, and small components are removed. For each remaining component, the row-wise pixel sum forms a horizontal projection: peaks mark candidate line centers, and a sufficiently deep valley between two peaks marks a cut position. Only the mask preparation, the cut shape, and the output representation depend on the ground-truth format. For binary line masks, the baseline prediction of ARU-Net~\cite{gruning2019arunet} is binarized with Otsu's method~\cite{otsu1979threshold}, thinned, and fused with the mask, and lines are separated along minimum-energy seams through background gaps. For PAGE XML, morphological closing first joins fragmented characters and words; lines are separated by straight horizontal cuts, and the components are clipped to the annotated main text region, filtered, and exported as PAGE XML polygons.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \input{graphics/semantic_gt_workflow}
    \caption{Semantic postprocessing by ground-truth format. For each component, \(q\) is the smoothed row projection and \(\rho\) is the valley-depth threshold. An accepted peak pair triggers either a minimum-energy seam or a horizontal cut. Gray blocks show processing steps; dashed blocks show output construction.}
    \label{fig:semantic-postprocessing}
\end{figure}

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
    \small
    \caption{Ground-truth-format-specific postprocessing of a semantic text-line probability map}
    \label{alg:semantic-postprocessing}
    \KwIn{Probability map \(P\), page image \(I\), ground-truth representation \(GT\), and main text-region mask \(R\), when available}
    \KwOut{Individual text-line components}

    \(M \leftarrow\) binarize \(P\) and remove small foreground components\;

    \eIf{\(GT=\text{binary mask}\)}{
        \(B \leftarrow\) apply Otsu thresholding to the ARU-Net baseline prediction for \(I\)\;
        Thin \(B\), combine it with \(M\), and remove small components\;
        \(C \leftarrow\) minimum-energy seam\;
    }{
        Join character and word fragments using morphological closing\;
        \(C \leftarrow\) straight horizontal cut\;
    }

    Find projection peaks and valleys in each connected component of \(M\)\;
    Separate merged lines through accepted valleys using \(C\)\;
    Remove small fragments created by the cuts\;

    \eIf{\(GT=\text{binary mask}\)}{
        \Return{the connected components of \(M\) as text-line instances}\;
    }{
        Clip \(M\) to \(R\)\;
        Discard components that are too small, too short, or insufficiently wide\;
        Convert the remaining components to polygons and baselines in PAGE XML\;
        \Return{the PAGE XML text-line polygons}\;
    }
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Two-Stage Detection and Segmentation}
\label{sec:two-stage-method}

The two-stage method separates line localization from boundary prediction (Figure~\ref{fig:two-stage-method}). An RT-DETR detector~\cite{Zhao_2024_CVPR} predicts one bounding box per text line on the complete page; the confidence threshold is selected on the validation split, and duplicate detections are removed. Each line thus receives its own candidate region, so neighboring lines cannot merge into one component.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \input{graphics/two_stage_gt_workflow}
    \caption{Two-stage text-line segmentation workflow. Solid arrows show inference, while dashed arrows show supervision used during training.}
    \label{fig:two-stage-method}
\end{figure}

A U-Net~\cite{DBLP:journals/corr/RonnebergerFB15} with a ResNet-34 encoder then segments each line crop. Its training targets are derived from the ground truth: each PAGE XML polygon, or each component of a binary mask, defines a bounding box, and the line mask inside this box forms the target; for binary masks, only the selected component is retained. The box is padded to preserve context, and crop and target are resized to a common elongated input size. In the detector-backbone comparison, the crop U-Net is kept fixed, so that differences are attributable to the detector.

The crop U-Net is trained on \(1024\times256\)-pixel crops with a padding of 15 pixels for 50 epochs, using AdamW with a learning rate of \(3\times10^{-4}\), a weight decay of \(10^{-4}\), and a batch size of four; the checkpoint with the highest validation Dice score is kept. Three segmentation losses are compared: binary cross-entropy (BCE), the Tversky loss~\cite{salehi2017tversky} with \(\alpha=0.3\) and \(\beta=0.7\), which weights missed foreground pixels more strongly than false positives, and the connectivity-preserving SuperVoxel loss~\cite{grim2025supervoxel} with \(\alpha=\beta=0.5\), which penalizes split and merged components. The Tversky and SuperVoxel models are warmed up with BCE for the first ten epochs. Unless stated otherwise, the crop U-Net is trained with the Tversky loss; the loss study is reported in Appendix~\ref{app:rq1-extra}.

At inference, each retained detection is padded in the same way and segmented independently. The probability map is resized to the native crop size before thresholding, and only its main connected component is kept. For binary-mask datasets, a horizontal morphological closing can additionally join fragmented line parts. The binarization threshold (0.5, 0.7, or 0.9) and the closing width (none or 4\% of the crop width) are selected per detector on the validation split by the mean line-level \gls{fm}, like the detection threshold. The crop masks are pasted into an empty page-sized instance map in descending confidence order, without overwriting pixels already assigned. For PAGE XML datasets, detections are restricted to the annotated main text region, overlapping row detections are removed, and a validation-selected width filter discards short detections. The largest contour of each crop mask becomes the text-line polygon, and the bottom of that polygon defines the baseline.

\subsection{Instance Segmentation}
\label{sec:mask-rcnn-method}

Mask R-CNN~\cite{he2017maskrcnn} treats every text line as a separate object of a single foreground class (Figure~\ref{fig:mask-rcnn-architecture}). A region proposal network generates candidate boxes; RoIAlign extracts aligned features for each proposal, from which parallel heads classify the proposal, refine its box, and predict its mask. Because every accepted proposal has its own mask, neighboring or overlapping lines remain separate.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \input{graphics/mask_rcnn_gt_workflow}
    \caption{Mask R-CNN workflow adapted from He et al.~\cite{he2017maskrcnn} to the evaluated backbones and ground-truth formats. (a) Both formats provide per-line boxes, masks, and labels for training. (b) Both use the detection and mask heads; for binary-mask datasets, the crop U-Net replaces the ROI masks before the instance map is assembled, whereas PAGE XML datasets produce polygons and baselines.}
    \label{fig:mask-rcnn-architecture}
\end{figure}

Each page is resized with preserved aspect ratio onto a white square canvas of \(704\times704\) pixels for DIVA-HisDB and \(1024\times1024\) pixels for U-DIADS-TL. Each Task~2 PAGE XML polygon of DIVA-HisDB, or each retained component of a U-DIADS-TL mask, yields one bounding box and one instance mask. Training augmentation is limited to brightness, contrast, saturation, and hue changes, so that the instance geometry remains aligned with the page.

The encoder feature maps (Section~\ref{sec:encoder-architectures}) feed a common 256-channel feature pyramid with identical proposal, box, and mask heads for all encoders; the COCO-pretrained ResNet-50 Mask R-CNN serves as an additional reference. The anchors use line-oriented aspect ratios. The box and mask predictors are reinitialized for the background and text-line classes, whereas the other heads keep their COCO initialization. The mask loss is binary cross-entropy, and the checkpoint is selected on the validation split.

At inference, detections are processed in descending confidence order, and their masks are restored to the original page resolution. On DIVA-HisDB, the confidence threshold is selected on the validation split by minimizing the absolute error of the predicted line count, and the mask threshold is fixed at 0.5. Each mask is clipped to the annotated main text region, its largest contour is exported as a PAGE XML polygon, and the lower edge of its bounding rectangle defines the baseline.

On U-DIADS-TL, the coarse ROI masks do not match the pixel-accurate binary ground truth. The ROI masks are therefore replaced by the crop U-Net of the two-stage method (Section~\ref{sec:two-stage-method}), while Mask R-CNN still supplies the proposals, their scores, and the instance identities. Each accepted box is padded, the crop is resized to \(1024\times256\) pixels and segmented, and the thresholded mask is pasted into the page-sized instance map without overwriting pixels of more confident instances. The confidence threshold is selected on the validation split by the box F1 score at an IoU of 0.5; the padding, the crop-mask threshold, and a minimum instance area are then selected on the validation split by \gls{fm}. Table~\ref{tab:rq1-maskrcnn-mask-vs-crop} compares these refined masks with the native ROI masks.

\section{Shot Selection Methods}
\label{sec:shot-sampling}

The shot-selection procedure chooses \(k\) pages from a pool of \(N\) training candidates. The six strategies comprise two baselines and four combinations of an embedding method and a geometric selection rule.

\paragraph{Baseline Strategies}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Grayscale variance} ranks each page \(I_i\) by
    \[
        v_i = \frac{1}{H_i W_i}
        \sum_{x=1}^{W_i}\sum_{y=1}^{H_i}
        \left(I_i(x,y)-\bar{I}_i\right)^2,
    \]
    where \(H_i\), \(W_i\), and \(\bar{I}_i\) denote its height, width, and mean intensity. The strategy selects the \(k\) largest scores.
    \item \emph{Random sampling} selects \(k\) pages without replacement from the lexicographically sorted candidates using seed 42.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Embedding Construction}
The remaining strategies use 512-dimensional full-page features \(\mathbf{z}_i\) from an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 without its classification layer~\cite{He_2016_CVPR}. Each page is resized to \(720\times720\) pixels and ImageNet-normalized, and each feature dimension is standardized across the candidate pool. Both methods below reduce the features from 512 to 2 dimensions, representing each page as a point \(\mathbf{y}_i \in \mathbb{R}^2\):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \Gls{pca} projects the features onto the two eigenvectors of the sample covariance matrix with the largest eigenvalues. These vectors form an orthonormal basis for the selected two-dimensional subspace, which retains the maximum possible variance. The resulting components are uncorrelated, but not necessarily statistically independent~\cite{Jolliffe2016PCA}.
    \item \Gls{ica}, computed with FastICA, seeks two statistically independent components by maximizing non-Gaussianity after whitening, which removes correlations and scales the retained components to unit variance. Its objective is independence rather than maximum retained variance; the resulting directions need not be orthogonal in the original feature space, and the components are not ordered by explained variance~\cite{Hyvarinen1999FastICA}. FastICA uses seed 42, at most 10,000 iterations, and a tolerance of \(10^{-5}\).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Selection Rules}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The \emph{max--min distance} rule selects the size-\(k\) subset with the largest minimum pairwise distance,
    \[
        S_{\mathrm{max\text{-}min}}^{*}
        = \underset{S \subseteq \{1,\ldots,N\},\,|S|=k}{\operatorname{arg\,max}}
          \;\underset{\substack{i,j\in S\\i\neq j}}{\operatorname{min}}
          \lVert \mathbf{y}_i-\mathbf{y}_j \rVert_2.
    \]
    For \(k=1\), all singleton scores are zero, so the first sorted page is returned.
    \item The \emph{farthest-from-centroid} rule selects the size-\(k\) subset with the largest total distance from the embedding centroid,
    \[
        \bar{\mathbf{y}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{y}_i,
        \qquad
        S_{\mathrm{centroid}}^{*}
        = \underset{S \subseteq \{1,\ldots,N\},\,|S|=k}{\operatorname{arg\,max}}
          \sum_{i\in S}\lVert\mathbf{y}_i-\bar{\mathbf{y}}\rVert_2.
    \]
    This is equivalent to selecting the \(k\) largest individual distances.
\end{itemize}
The max--min rule maximizes mutual separation, whereas the centroid rule selects peripheral points independently. 
Figures~\ref{fig:shot-sampling-pca} and~\ref{fig:shot-sampling-ica} illustrate both rules for \(k=5\). Page identifiers mark pages selected by at least one rule.

\section{Cross-Generalization Methods}
\label{sec:cross-generalization}

RQ3 examines whether a model trained on a few pages of one or more collections transfers to a collection that is never seen during training. It uses the seven cross-collection datasets of Section~\ref{sec:datasets}.

\subsection{Collection Partitions}

Each collection is partitioned once with seed 42 (Table~\ref{tab:dataset-splits}): three pages are drawn for training, 10\% of the pages, rounded to the nearest integer, for validation, and the remaining pages form the test set. Pinkas and NorHand v3 keep their official test sets and draw their training and validation pages from the official training part, which for NorHand v3 is a random subset of 30 pages. For NorHand v3, a first draw of three training pages did not represent the test pages and was replaced by a second draw from the same pool (Appendix~\ref{app:rq3-extra}). All line polygons are converted to instance masks, and pages with missing polygons or polygons with fewer than three vertices are excluded.

\subsection{Model and Training}

All experiments use the Mask R-CNN of Section~\ref{sec:mask-rcnn-method}, retaining the CATMuS-pretrained proposal network and applying scale jitter during training. Table~\ref{tab:rq3-training-settings} lists the model, augmentation, and optimization settings. The configuration is selected by mean source-validation \gls{fm} in the ablation of Appendix~\ref{app:rq3-extra}. Score and mask thresholds are selected by validation \gls{fm}, with \gls{pixeliu} as the tie-breaker.

\subsection{Transfer Protocols}

Three experiments are conducted:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Zero-shot.} The CATMuS-pretrained model is applied without fine-tuning, using the fixed thresholds in Table~\ref{tab:rq3-training-settings}.
    \item \textbf{Protocol~A.} One model is trained on the three training pages of each collection, its thresholds are selected on the validation pages of that collection, and it is applied unchanged to all seven test sets. The diagonal of the resulting \(7\times7\) matrix is the in-domain result, and the off-diagonal cells measure zero-shot transfer.
    \item \textbf{Protocol~B.} For each held-out collection, a \gls{loco} model is trained on \(k\in\{1,2,3\}\) pages from each of the six other collections, that is, on 6, 12, or 18 pages (\gls{loco}-1, \gls{loco}-2, and \gls{loco}-3). The page sets are nested and taken from the protocol-A training pages. The thresholds are selected on up to three validation pages per source collection, so the held-out collection takes no part in training or selection. The result is compared with the in-domain result and with the best off-diagonal protocol-A entry of the same column, which is an optimistic reference because it is identified on test results.
\end{itemize}
