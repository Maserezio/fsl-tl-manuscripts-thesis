\chapter{Background and Related Work}
\label{lit_rec}

This chapter describes \gls{tls} within the \gls{hda} pipeline, then summarizes segmentation methods by the learning paradigm, architecture, and line representation. Next, it describes the \gls{fsl} paradigm, reviews its application to \gls{tls}, describes feature extractors and prior strategies, and ends with the research gap.

\section{Historical Document Analysis}

\Gls{hda} is a research field focused on digitizing, processing, and extracting information from historical documents, whether for content extraction or metadata analysis, such as writer identification.

\section{Text Line Segmentation}
\label{sec:tls}

Text line segmentation aims to identify and separate individual text lines in a historical document image. Depending on the labeling method, a text line can be represented as a bounding box, a set of foreground pixels, a polygon surrounding the line, or a baseline. Figure~\ref{fig:line_representation} illustrates how different representations describe the same handwritten text lines \cite{Textline_maskrcnn}.

	\caption{Representations of a text line, reproduced from Islam et al. \cite{Textline_maskrcnn}.}

\subsection{Unsupervised Methods}
\label{sec:related-unsupervised}

Unsupervised methods extract individual lines without manually annotated training data, which is particularly useful when labeled data for historical documents is limited. The most recent works rely on handcrafted heuristics applied to document structure, such as including elongation analysis for line slicing \cite{sathik2021}, seam carving with specifically designed cost functions \cite{nguyen2022effective}, statistical gap analysis with unsupervised parameter estimation \cite{mukherjee2022unsupervised}, and clustering-based blob-line generation combined with energy minimization \cite{droby2022unsupervised}. Although unsupervised models are a minority among recently released models, as Rabaev et al. \cite{Rabaev_Litvak_2025} report, they achieve performance comparable to, and sometimes better than, state-of-the-art models, as shown in Table~\ref{tab:unsupervised_diva_metrics}. However, their strong performance sometimes depends on the specific assumptions and characteristics of the document collections they are developed for, thus raising the question of whether such methods are robust. Hu et al. \cite{hu2021uchen} propose a method targeted specifically at Uchen Tibetan documents. In contrast, Nguyen et al. \cite{nguyen2022effective} and Droby et al. \cite{droby2022unsupervised} report strong, consistent results across different manuscript families. Additionally, these methods are usually computationally heavy, taking up to approximately 30 minutes per page of processing, depending on parameter configuration \cite{DBLP:journals/corr/abs-2003-08632}.

\subsection{Supervised Methods}
\label{sec:related-supervised}

Supervised methods learn text line representations directly from annotated data. Rabaev et al.~\cite{Rabaev_Litvak_2025} review 49 peer-reviewed studies published between 2019 and 2024 and report 40 as supervised or hybrid. These methods usually fall into two approaches: semantic segmentation, which predicts a pixel-level map of the page (e.g., text, baselines, or other line geometry) and recovers individual lines in a postprocessing step, or instance segmentation, which predicts a separate region for each line. Recent work has shifted toward instance segmentation models. Recent work has shifted toward instance segmentation models~\cite{Rabaev_Litvak_2025}. Fizaine et al.~\cite{fizaine} compared both approaches on the same data and found the instance segmentation model more effective: Mask R-CNN detected the lines with an \gls{ap}@0.5 of 96\%, compared with 62\% for the semantic segmentation network Doc-UFCN~\cite{boillet2021docufcn}.

\subsubsection{Semantic Segmentation}
\label{sec:related-segm}

Supervised segmentation models learn pixel prediction from annotated pages. Fully convolutional networks such as Doc-UFCN \cite{boillet2021docufcn} and dhSegment \cite{oliveira2018dhsegment} predict foreground pixels and recover lines from connected components; neighboring or overlapping lines may then form one foreground region and require an additional separation step. APAU-Net \cite{Beghoura2026-dp} keeps the pixel prediction but adds geometric priors. Other models predict line geometry instead of a mask: ARU-Net \cite{gruning2019arunet} detects baselines, and the system of Kodym and Hradi\v{s} \cite{kodym2021page} predicts baselines along with the vertical extent of the lines, from which the line regions are reconstructed. 

\subsubsection{Instance Segmentation}
\label{sec:related-detection}

Instance-based methods predict a separate region for every line. Mask R-CNN \cite{he2017maskrcnn} detects one box per line and predicts a mask inside it. Islam et al. \cite{Textline_maskrcnn} apply it across scripts, and Mask R-CNN+ \cite{signals3030032} reports the best published line \gls{fm} of 97.43\% on DIVA-HisDB. Jindal and Ghosh \cite{jindal2023fasterrcnn} detect line boxes with Faster R-CNN. In Mask R-CNN, the mask of a line is predicted on a fixed \(28\times28\) grid inside its box \cite{he2017maskrcnn}, which is coarse for long, thin text lines. 

\subsection{Generalization Across Collections}

Generalization across different scripts and document collections has been evaluated comparatively rarely. Islam et al. \cite{Textline_maskrcnn} study cross-script generalization by training their Mask R-CNN model only on the Urdu HUP dataset and evaluating it on eight other scripts. The model performs reasonably well on documents with layouts similar to the training data, but its performance drops by roughly 20--30\% on datasets with layouts the authors claim are non-similar. The largest failures occur on Arabic and German documents with very low inter-line spacing, showing that differences in page structure can still considerably affect cross-script generalization. Boillet et al.~\cite{Boillet2022} investigate cross-dataset robustness by
training Doc-UFCN~\cite{boillet2021docufcn},
dhSegment~\cite{oliveira2018dhsegment}, and
ARU-Net~\cite{gruning2019arunet} on a unified collection of ten
historical document datasets and evaluating on the previously unseen collections. Their results
demonstrate that training on heterogeneous data can support
generalization to unseen pages. 

\subsection{Learning with Few Annotated Pages}
\label{sec:related-fewshot}

Learning with few annotated pages has only recently become a research direction. Zottin et al. \cite{zottin2025exploring} first evaluated three established semantic segmentation architectures---\gls{fcn} \cite{long2015fully}, \gls{pspnet} \cite{zhao2017pyramid}, and DeepLabV3+ \cite{chen2018encoder}---on the U-DIADS-TL dataset, with only 3 annotated images. Their low scores indicated potential for improvement, as this was the first baseline study in this paradigm.

The FEST competition \cite{Zottin_2025_FEST} compared task-specific solutions on U-DIADS-TL, which outperformed the standard baselines. The winning PERO system reached a line \gls{fm} of 83.0\%. It used a ParseNet model \cite{kodym2021page} trained on the PERO layout dataset, which predicts baselines and the distances to the upper and lower edges of each line, so individual lines are constructed from the predicted line geometry. The line masks were then obtained by adaptive thresholding within the text regions, rendering the baselines to connect fragments, morphological dilation, and removing small components. Sterzinger et al. \cite{sterzinger2025fewshot} train a lightweight UNet++ model with a connection-aware loss with a \gls{lineiu} ogbtaibni 0.774 F-measure on U-DIADS-TL. More recently, APAU-Net \cite{Beghoura2026-dp} combines a residual U-Net with geometric priors and reaches an average \gls{fm} of 0.634 under the same three-page training setting.

However, these recent few-shot text line segmentation studies have been developed and evaluated only for the U-DIADS-TL dataset and have not been evaluated on other datasets. This leaves the behavior of few-shot \gls{tls} methods across different historical document collections comparatively underexplored.

These developments motivate describing the \gls{fsl} paradigm in the next chapter

\section{Few-Shot Learning}
\label{sec:fsl}

\Gls{fsl} aims to learn from a small amount of labeled data \cite{fsl} where the number of training instances, called shots \(k\), typically \(k \in \{1,3,5,10\}\). In the \gls{hda} domain, this paradigm is highly relevant because of data limitations and the cost of labeling new data. De Nardin et al. \cite{De_Nardin_2023} proposed a few-shot framework in an adjacent task of historical document layout analysis using only six annotated pages, two from each manuscript, instead of the complete 60-page training set of DIVA-HisDB \cite{diva_hisdb}. The data-efficient method achieved performance comparable to approaches that use the full training data. Additionally, for scene text segmentation in natural images, Li et al. \cite{li2025tsalfewshottextsegmentation} proposed a model that creates prior knowledge from the pretrained \gls{clip} model \cite{DBLP:journals/corr/abs-2103-00020} to learn text attributes from only a few labeled images. On the TextSeg dataset \cite{xu2020rethinkingtextsegmentationnovel}, their 4-shot setting outperforms several many-shot baselines trained with up to 128 labeled examples, demonstrating the potential of few-shot methods for text segmentation, even though the work is outside the \gls{hda} domain. \Gls{fsl} can be applied in two approaches: meta-learning, where a model is trained to adapt rapidly to new tasks from a small support set, and transfer learning, where knowledge learned from a larger source dataset is reused for a target task with limited labeled data \cite{fsl}.

\subsection{Transfer Learning and Pretraining}

In deep neural networks, transfer learning is commonly implemented by initializing the model's feature extractor with pretrained weights and then adapting these weights to the target task through a brief training process called fine-tuning \cite{plested2026transfer}. This approach lets the feature extractor adjust representations learned from a larger source dataset rather than learning them entirely from limited target data.

This approach is available with both \glspl{cnn} and transformer-based encoders. ResNet-derived \glspl{cnn} have been adopted as generic feature extractors, whereas \glspl{vit} have demonstrated state-of-the-art performance on large-scale computer vision benchmarks \cite{dosovitskiy2021imageworth16x16words}. Further work on data-efficient training strategies enhanced \gls{vit} performance under limited data constraints \cite{touvron2021trainingdataefficientimagetransformers}. In parallel, ConvNeXt established that appropriately modernized convolutional architectures can achieve performance comparable to transformer-based approaches \cite{woo2023convnextv2}. Later, self-supervised vision foundation models pretrained in \gls{ssl} paradigm have provided general-purpose pretrained representations. For instance, DINOv2 pretrains \gls{vit} encoders on the LVD-142M dataset without labels, and its features transfer well to dense prediction tasks \cite{oquab2024dinov2learningrobustvisual}. DINOv3 extends this family to both \gls{vit} and ConvNeXt encoders, and knowledge distillation from it has outperformed standard supervised pretraining on dense tasks such as semantic segmentation \cite{dinov3}.

Experiments with different feature extractors are also reported in \gls{hda}. Wödlinger and Sablatnig use an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 as the encoder of a U-Net-based baseline detection system and report an F1 score of 0.881 on cBAD 2019 \cite{wodlinger2021baseline}. Their experiments further show that improving the initial phase of model estimation improves final performance, with the F1 score increasing by approximately 10\% to 0.963 when ground-truth start points and angles are provided. Jindal and Ghosh \cite{jindal2023fasterrcnn} further replaced the conventional VGG-16 backbone of Faster R-CNN with ResNet-50 for ancient handwritten text line segmentation and report an F-measure of 99.98\%, outperforming the original Faster R-CNN configuration. Fizaine et al. \cite{fizaine} also suggest replacing the backbone in their Mask R-CNN implementation with a more recent architecture, such as ConvNeXt \cite{woo2023convnextv2}, to potentially improve both accuracy and processing speed. These works suggest room for extensive research on \gls{tls} backbones. 

Beyond the architecture, the pretraining source also matters. Domain-specific pretraining has been shown to improve document analysis: in layout analysis, De Nardin et al. \cite{denardin2025indomain} raise the \gls{iou} on CB55 from 43.9\% to 54.6\% and the F1 from 57.6\% to 69.0\% with hybrid \gls{coco}+U-DIADS-Bib pretraining compared with training from scratch. For \gls{tls}, however, it has rarely been studied. Common backbones are pretrained on large datasets such as ImageNet \cite{deng2009imagenet}, whereas historical document datasets such as \gls{cbad} \cite{cbad-2019} and CATMuS \cite{catmus-medieval} are two to four orders of magnitude smaller but closer to the target domain (Table~\ref{tab:pretraining_datasets}). Pretraining on such manuscript data, with or without line annotations, may therefore also improve \gls{tls}, but fine-tuning on a few pages can also reduce the quality gained during pretraining. The \gls{wiseft} method prevents this by interpolating the weights of the pretrained and the fine-tuned model, which improves model robustness under distribution shift without additional training~\cite{wortsman2022wiseft}.

\section{Summary of Research Gap}
After reviewing related work, several gaps for few-shot \gls{tls} in historical manuscripts were identified.

Few studies examine how the feature extractor influences \gls{tls}, and those that do use older backbones. Recent encoders, distillation from vision foundation models, and domain-specific pretraining have therefore not been compared under the same setting. Moreover, the reviewed methods obtain individual lines in different ways, but the contribution of this step has rarely been evaluated separately from the image features.

Additionally, few-shot \gls{tls} has been evaluated only on U-DIADS-TL with three training pages, so the number of pages needed on other benchmarks is unknown. It is also unclear whether a dedicated selection strategy should choose the training pages or whether random selection is sufficient. Finally, transfer across collections has been studied only for models trained on full datasets, so it is unknown how the diversity of the training collections affects transfer with few annotated pages.

This thesis addresses these gaps with three research questions. \gls{rq}~1 compares encoders, initializations, and line reconstruction in two pipeline types, \gls{rq}~2 varies the number and selection of training pages, and \gls{rq}~3 evaluates transfer across seven manuscript collections.
