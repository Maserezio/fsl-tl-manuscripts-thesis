\chapter{Related Work}
\label{lit_rec}

This chapter describes \gls{tls} within \gls{hda}, then summarizes segmentation methods by the learning paradigm, architecture, and line representation. Next, it describes the \gls{fsl} paradigm, reviews its application to \gls{tls}, and describes feature extractors and prior strategies.

\section{Text Line Segmentation in Historical Manuscripts}
\label{sec:tls}
\Gls{hda} is a research field focused on digitizing, processing, and extracting information from historical documents, whether for content extraction or metadata analysis, such as writer identification.

\Gls{hda} typically involves multiple stages, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:da_steps}, and machine learning is used at each of them \cite{jimaging11050133, De_Nardin_2023, Li2025}. Historical materials introduce several challenges because their visual appearance and layout differ across and within collections. Usual issues include uneven line spacing, different text orientations, notes in the margins, stains, bleed-through, and characters or text lines that touch or overlap \cite{Rabaev_Litvak_2025}. Figure~\ref{fig:hda_challenges} shows general layout- and degradation-related problems, along with examples of overlapping and deteriorated text line regions.

As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:da_steps}, text line segmentation is the last step in the preprocessing phase, before \gls{htr}. Segmentation quality therefore affects downstream \gls{htr} performance. Fizaine et al.~\cite{fizaine} measured this effect by comparing two segmentation models with the same TrOCR \cite{DBLP:journals/corr/abs-2109-10282} recognizer, fine-tuned on the DRoSb dataset, which they also used to train the segmentation models. Since the \gls{htr} model is identical, the difference in \gls{cer} is caused by the segmentation. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:fizaine_ap_htr}, the Mask R-CNN \cite{he2017maskrcnn} model has better object-level metrics and also a lower \gls{cer}.

Depending on the task, a text line can be represented as a bounding box, a set of foreground pixels, a polygon surrounding the line, or a baseline. \subsection{Unsupervised Methods}
\label{sec:related-unsupervised}

Unsupervised methods extract individual lines without manually annotated training data, which is particularly useful when labeled data for historical documents is limited. The most recent unsupervised methods rely on handcrafted heuristics applied to document structure, such as elongation analysis for line slicing \cite{sathik2021}, seam carving with specifically designed cost functions \cite{nguyen2022effective}, and statistical gap analysis with unsupervised parameter estimation \cite{mukherjee2022unsupervised}. Others train a deep network without annotations and combine it with blob-line generation and energy minimization \cite{droby2022unsupervised}. Although unsupervised models are a minority among recently released models, as Rabaev et al. \cite{Rabaev_Litvak_2025} report, they achieve performance comparable to state-of-the-art models. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:unsupervised_diva_metrics}, on DIVA-HisDB, two unsupervised methods reach a higher \gls{lineiu} and \gls{pixeliu} than Mask R-CNN. However, their performance can depend on assumptions about the document collection they are developed for, which raises the question of whether such methods are robust. For example, Hu et al. \cite{hu2021uchen} propose a method evaluated only on Uchen Tibetan documents. In contrast, Nguyen et al. \cite{nguyen2022effective} and Droby et al. \cite{droby2022unsupervised} report strong, consistent results across different manuscript families.

\subsection{Supervised Methods}
\label{sec:related-supervised}

Supervised methods learn text line representations directly from annotated data. Rabaev et al.~\cite{Rabaev_Litvak_2025} review 49 peer-reviewed studies published between 2019 and 2024 and report 40 as supervised or hybrid. These methods usually fall into two approaches: semantic segmentation, which predicts a pixel-level map of the page (e.g., text, baselines, or other line geometry) and recovers individual lines in a postprocessing step \cite{boillet2021docufcn, kodym2021page, Boillet2022, fizaine}, or instance segmentation, which predicts a separate region for each line \cite{Textline_maskrcnn, signals3030032, fizaine, li2023dtdt, agrawal2024linetr}. Fizaine et al.~\cite{fizaine} compared both approaches on the same data and found the instance segmentation model more effective: Mask R-CNN detected the lines with an \gls{ap}@0.5 of 96\%, compared with 62\% for the semantic segmentation network Doc-UFCN~\cite{boillet2021docufcn}.

\subsubsection{Semantic Segmentation}
\label{sec:related-segm}

Supervised segmentation models learn pixel prediction from annotated pages. \Glspl{fcn} such as Doc-UFCN \cite{boillet2021docufcn} and dhSegment \cite{oliveira2018dhsegment} predict foreground pixels and recover lines from connected components; neighboring or overlapping lines may then form one foreground region and require an additional separation step. APAU-Net \cite{Beghoura2026-dp} keeps the pixel prediction but adds geometric priors. Other models predict line geometry instead of a mask: ARU-Net \cite{gruning2019arunet} detects baselines, and the system of Kodym and Hradi\v{s} \cite{kodym2021page} predicts baselines along with the vertical extent of the lines, from which the line regions are reconstructed. Wödlinger and Sablatnig \cite{wodlinger2021baseline} predict baselines together with the start points of the lines and reconstruct each line by tracing its baseline from the start point with a recurrent convolutional network. On cBAD 2019, they report an F1 score of 0.881, which increases to 0.963 when the ground-truth start points and angles are provided. Their results show that the line reconstruction impacts their model performance.

\subsubsection{Instance Segmentation}
\label{sec:related-detection}

Instance-based methods predict a separate region for every line. Mask R-CNN \cite{he2017maskrcnn} detects one box per line and predicts a mask inside it. Islam et al. \cite{Textline_maskrcnn} apply it across scripts, and Mask R-CNN+ \cite{signals3030032} reports the best published line \gls{fm} of 97.43\% on DIVA-HisDB. Jindal and Ghosh \cite{jindal2023fasterrcnn} detect line boxes with Faster R-CNN. Transformer-based detectors have also been applied to text lines. Li et al.~\cite{li2023dtdt} detect densely written lines in historical Chinese documents with a DETR-style model, and LineTR~\cite{agrawal2024linetr} combines a DETR-style detector with seam-based line polygons for palm-leaf manuscripts.

\subsection{Generalization Across Collections}

Of the studies reviewed in this chapter, only two evaluate the generalization of \gls{tls} models to other scripts and document collections. Islam et al. \cite{Textline_maskrcnn} study cross-script generalization by training their Mask R-CNN model only on the Urdu HUP dataset~\cite{Textline_maskrcnn} and evaluating it on other scripts. On datasets whose layout the authors consider similar to the training data, the model reaches a mean \gls{lineiu} of 79.7\%, compared with 64.7\% on datasets with dissimilar layouts, such as DIVA-HisDB. The largest failures occur on Arabic and German documents with low inter-line spacing, which shows that differences in page structure affect cross-script generalization. Boillet et al.~\cite{Boillet2022} investigate cross-dataset robustness by
training Doc-UFCN~\cite{boillet2021docufcn},
dhSegment~\cite{oliveira2018dhsegment}, and
ARU-Net~\cite{gruning2019arunet} on a unified collection of ten
historical document datasets and evaluating on the previously unseen collections. Their results
demonstrate that training on heterogeneous data can support
generalization to unseen pages. 

\subsection{Few-Shot Text Line Segmentation}
\label{sec:related-fewshot}

Learning with few annotated pages has only recently become a research direction. Zottin et al. \cite{zottin2025exploring} first evaluated three established semantic segmentation architectures---\gls{fcn} \cite{long2015fully}, \gls{pspnet} \cite{zhao2017pyramid}, and DeepLabV3+ \cite{chen2018encoder}---on the U-DIADS-TL dataset, with only three annotated images. Their low scores indicated potential for improvement, as this was the first baseline study in this paradigm.

The FEST competition \cite{Zottin_2025_FEST} compared task-specific solutions on U-DIADS-TL, which outperformed the standard baselines. The winning PERO system reached a line \gls{fm} of 83.0\%. It used a ParseNet model \cite{kodym2021page} trained on the PERO layout dataset, which predicts baselines and the distances to the upper and lower edges of each line, so individual lines are constructed from the predicted line geometry. The line masks were then obtained by adaptive thresholding within the text regions, rendering the baselines to connect fragments, morphological dilation, and removing small components. Sterzinger et al. \cite{sterzinger2025fewshot} train a lightweight U-Net++ model with a connection-aware loss and obtain a line \gls{fm} of 61.40\% on U-DIADS-TL. With three training pages per manuscript, they also evaluate baseline detection on DIVA-HisDB. APAU-Net \cite{Beghoura2026-dp} combines a residual U-Net with geometric priors and reaches an average \gls{fm} of 63.4\% under the same three-page training setting.

However, these few-shot text line segmentation studies evaluate line regions only on U-DIADS-TL, and on DIVA-HisDB only baseline detection has been evaluated with three training pages \cite{sterzinger2025fewshot}. The behavior of few-shot \gls{tls} methods across different historical document collections has therefore not been evaluated.

\section{Few-Shot Learning}
\label{sec:fsl}

\Gls{fsl} aims to learn from a small amount of labeled data \cite{fsl} where the number of training instances, called shots \(k\), typically \(k \in \{1,3,5,10\}\). In the \gls{hda} domain, this paradigm is relevant because of data limitations and the cost of labeling new data \cite{sterzinger2025fewshot, Zottin_2025_FEST}. De Nardin et al. \cite{De_Nardin_2023} proposed a few-shot framework in an adjacent task of historical document layout analysis using only six annotated pages, two from each manuscript, instead of the complete 60-page training set of DIVA-HisDB \cite{diva_hisdb}. This method achieved performance comparable to approaches that use the full training data. Additionally, for scene text segmentation in natural images, Li et al. \cite{li2025tsalfewshottextsegmentation} proposed a model that creates prior knowledge from the pretrained \gls{clip} model \cite{DBLP:journals/corr/abs-2103-00020} to learn text attributes from only a few labeled images. On the TextSeg dataset \cite{xu2020rethinkingtextsegmentationnovel}, their 4-shot setting outperforms many-shot baselines trained with up to 128 labeled examples, demonstrating the potential of few-shot methods for text segmentation, even though the work is outside the \gls{hda} domain. \Gls{fsl} can be applied in two approaches: meta-learning, where a model is trained to adapt rapidly to new tasks from a small support set, and transfer learning, where knowledge learned from a larger source dataset is reused for a target task with limited labeled data \cite{fsl}. Since this thesis addresses a single task and does not adapt the model to new tasks, it uses transfer learning only.

\subsection{Transfer Learning Overview}

In deep neural networks, transfer learning is commonly implemented by initializing the model's feature extractor with pretrained weights and then adapting these weights to the target task through a training process called fine-tuning \cite{plested2026transfer}. This approach lets the feature extractor adjust representations learned from a larger source dataset rather than learning them entirely from limited target data.

This approach is available with both \glspl{cnn} and transformer-based encoders. ResNet-derived \glspl{cnn} \cite{He_2016_CVPR} have been adopted as generic feature extractors. \Glspl{vit} \cite{dosovitskiy2021imageworth16x16words} have demonstrated state-of-the-art performance on large-scale computer vision benchmarks. DeiT \cite{touvron2021trainingdataefficientimagetransformers} introduced data-efficient training strategies that improve \gls{vit} performance when no additional pretraining data is used. In parallel, ConvNeXt \cite{liu2022convnet2020s} established that appropriately modernized convolutional architectures can achieve performance comparable to transformer-based approaches. Later, vision foundation models pretrained with \gls{ssl} have provided general-purpose pretrained representations. For instance, DINOv2 \cite{oquab2024dinov2learningrobustvisual} pretrains \gls{vit} encoders on the LVD-142M dataset without labels, and its frozen features can be used for dense prediction tasks. DINOv3 \cite{dinov3} extends this family to both \gls{vit} and ConvNeXt encoders, and knowledge distillation from it has outperformed standard supervised pretraining on dense tasks such as semantic segmentation.

Of the \gls{tls} studies reviewed in this chapter, only two consider replacing the encoder. Jindal and Ghosh \cite{jindal2023fasterrcnn} replaced the conventional VGG-16 backbone of Faster R-CNN with ResNet-50 for ancient handwritten text line segmentation and report an F-measure of 99.98\%, outperforming the original Faster R-CNN configuration. Fizaine et al. \cite{fizaine} suggest replacing the backbone in their Mask R-CNN implementation with a more recent architecture, such as ConvNeXt \cite{liu2022convnet2020s}, to potentially improve both accuracy and processing speed, but they do not test this replacement.

Pretraining on document images is also used for \gls{tls}. Mechi et al.~\cite{mechi2021twostep} pretrain \glspl{fcn} on 2,499 images of the READ dataset and fine-tune them on each target dataset, including DIVA-HisDB. Baloun et al.~\cite{baloun2025ssl} pretrain an encoder with \gls{ssl} on historical handwritten documents and report that its features are useful for document segmentation tasks, particularly when only a few labeled pages are available. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:pretraining_datasets}, such document datasets, for example \gls{cbad} 2019 \cite{cbad-2019} and CATMuS Medieval \cite{catmus-medieval}, are two to four orders of magnitude smaller than ImageNet \cite{deng2009imagenet} but closer to the target domain.

Beyond the architecture, the pretraining source also matters. Domain-specific pretraining has been shown to improve document analysis: in layout analysis, De Nardin et al. \cite{denardin2025indomain} raise the \gls{iou} on CB55 from 43.9\% to 54.6\% and the F1 from 57.6\% to 69.0\% with hybrid \gls{coco}+U-DIADS-Bib pretraining compared with training from scratch. This result concerns layout analysis. For \gls{tls}, Boillet et al.~\cite{boillet2021docufcn} show that pretraining on several document datasets improves text line detection, but no study was found that compares generic pretraining, such as ImageNet, with domain-specific pretraining of the same encoder. Additionally, fine-tuning can reduce the robustness to distribution shifts that the pretrained model had \cite{wortsman2022wiseft}, and with a few pages, the fine-tuned model may lose part of what it learned during pretraining. The \gls{wiseft} method prevents this by interpolating the weights of the pretrained and the fine-tuned model, which improves model robustness under distribution shift without additional training~\cite{wortsman2022wiseft}.
