\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}\label{sec:disc-qualitative}\label{sec:results-qualitative}

This chapter analyzes the errors of the transfer to unseen collections, relates the results of Chapter~\ref{ch:results} across the \glspl{rq}, and states the limitations of this thesis.

\section{Line Size and Spacing in Transfer}
\label{sec:disc-transfer-errors}\label{sec:results-transfer-diagnostics}

Without fine-tuning, the transfer of Mask R-CNN depends on the size of the lines, as could be seen from table 5.1. At the model input, the median height of a line polygon ranges from 12.5 pixels on Pinkas to 39.4 pixels on GRPOLY-DB, and the larger the lines are, the more of them Mask R-CNN splits into parts. On Pinkas, it predicts 1.26 lines per \gls{gt} line, while on GRPOLY-DB, it predicts 5.76 lines per \gls{gt} line and reaches 0.07\%. Over the seven collections, the Spearman correlation between the line height and the \gls{fm} of Mask R-CNN without fine-tuning is $-0.82$.

Figure~\ref{fig:rq3-zeroshot-grpoly} shows that the splits occur at the gaps between words. Mask R-CNN divides several long lines into parts. On a GRPOLY-DB page with 5.50 predicted lines per \gls{gt} line, it segments single words instead of lines and reaches 0\%. As described in Section~\ref{sec:rq3-center-line}, the center-line model rescales every page so that its lines have the thickness \(T_0\) before decoding and returns every line of both pages as one polygon. It reaches more than 84\% on every collection, including the Hebrew, Arabic, Greek, and Syriac scripts that do not occur in CATMuS.

After fine-tuning on the target collection, the remaining errors are mainly merges of neighboring lines on dense pages. Table~\ref{tab:rq3-line-density} relates the transfer to the line density of the test pages. Phil.\ gr.\ 130 and Pinkas are the densest collections. The line density limits Mask R-CNN, whose lowest transfer occurs on Phil.\ gr.\ 130. The center-line model is insensitive to the density and reaches 94.52\% on Phil.\ gr.\ 130. Its lowest target collection, Pinkas, has the same ratio of pitch to line height as RASAM, 0.69, but reaches 85.13\% compared with 93.89\%. Its errors on Pinkas come from the layout rather than from the density. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:rq3-pinkas-margin}, the \gls{gt} annotates the entry numbers in the margin as separate lines, which the center-line model joins to the neighboring text line. On Phil.\ gr.\ 130, the merges of Mask R-CNN are in the mask head. When the \gls{gt} boxes are passed to the mask head of the model trained on ONB Cod.\ Syr.\ 1, the line \gls{fm} on the thirteen Phil.\ gr.\ 130 test pages drops from 53.3\% to 44.2\%, while the \gls{gt} masks exported in the same way reach 100\%.

On the training and validation pages of Phil.\ gr.\ 130, Pinkas, and NorHand v3, a \gls{gt} line counts as merged if one predicted instance covers at least 25\% of its ink and of the ink of another line. As could be seen in Table~\ref{tab:rq3-error-types}, the two models differ in the merges, with 33.3\% of the lines merged by Mask R-CNN and 9.5\% by the center-line model. The difference is largest on Phil.\ gr.\ 130 and smallest on NorHand v3, and for both models, merges become more frequent as the distance to the neighboring line decreases. Figure~\ref{fig:rq3-phil-merges} shows such merges on a Phil.\ gr.\ 130 test page for the models trained on ONB Cod.\ Syr.\ 1. Mask R-CNN joins neighboring lines into one polygon and reaches 62.69\% on this page, while the center-line model separates them and reaches 98.85\%.

The transfer matrices also show whether the source or the target collection determines the result. In an additive model of the 42 off-diagonal cells of each matrix, the target collection alone explains 83\% of the variance for the center-line model and the source collection 3\%. For Mask R-CNN, the target collection explains 48\%, and 32\% of the variance remain specific to the pair of collections. Part of this pair effect follows from the line size, since the transfer of Mask R-CNN decreases as the line heights of the source and the target collection differ more, with a Spearman correlation of $-0.46$.

\section{Synthesis}
\label{sec:disc-synthesis}

As described in Section~\ref{sec:disc-reconstruction}, the encoder architecture has the smallest effect of the studied factors, because most errors arise after the encoder, in the step that turns a prediction into individual lines. The encoders give similar predictions, and the remaining errors come from coarse masks that extend beyond the ink. The best published system on U-DIADS-TL follows the same principle. Kodym and Hradi\v{s}~\cite{kodym2021page} derive the lines from a predicted line geometry instead of a single mask, and their ParseNet, trained on the PERO layout data, gives the best result of the FEST competition~\cite{Zottin_2025_FEST}. The results of this thesis indicate that, with a few annotated pages, this reconstruction step determines the result more than the capacity of the encoder.

Pretraining on annotated lines makes three training pages sufficient. As described in Section~\ref{sec:disc-pretraining}, the CATMuS-pretrained Mask R-CNN finds most DIVA-HisDB lines without fine-tuning, and one training page mainly raises its line precision. The few pages therefore adapt the model to the annotation convention of the benchmark, for example to exclude interlinear glosses. Features learned from manuscript pages without line annotations do not have this effect, as the cBAD-distilled encoders reach lower results than CATMuS with one page. The model therefore builds on the line annotations of the pretraining rather than on the domain of its images. This agrees with Tian et al.~\cite{tian2020rethinking}, for whom a good pretrained representation matters more than the few-shot learning method, and with De Nardin et al.~\cite{De_Nardin_2023}, who reach layout segmentation on DIVA-HisDB with two annotated pages per manuscript.

The same adaptation to the annotation convention also explains part of the transfer to other collections. Fine-tuning on three pages of one collection adapts a model to the conventions of this collection and can reduce the robustness that it had after pretraining~\cite{wortsman2022wiseft}. As shown in Appendix~\ref{app:rq3-wiseft}, interpolating the fine-tuned weights with the pretrained ones by \gls{wiseft} decreases the transfer of the center-line model, whose pretrained weights transfer best. For Mask R-CNN, the interpolation increases the transfer, but its transfer remains limited by other factors. As described in Section~\ref{sec:disc-transfer-errors}, these factors are the size and the spacing of the lines. Islam et al.~\cite{Textline_maskrcnn} reach the same conclusion with complete training sets. Their Mask R-CNN, trained only on Urdu pages, transfers to other scripts, and its lower scores on Arabic, German, and Medieval Latin pages are caused by the lower interline spacing of these collections. In the few-shot transfer of this thesis, the size and the density of the lines also limit Mask R-CNN more than the script.

These results suggest three practical rules for a new manuscript collection. First, a model pretrained on annotated lines needs about three annotated pages per manuscript. With CATMuS initialization, Mask R-CNN reaches 98.62\% with three pages, while with ImageNet initialization it reaches 98.35\% only with ten pages, which saves seven annotated pages per manuscript. Second, if no page of the target collection can be annotated, a model that represents lines by their center lines transfers better than a detector. Third, a detector should be trained on source collections with a similar line size as the target collection, and on several of them rather than one.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:disc-limitations}

The cBAD distillation used shorter schedules than recommended because of limited computational resources. Distillation with the recommended schedules requires no additional data, only more computation, and may give a stronger domain-specific encoder. The configuration of the transfer models and the parameters of the ink-ownership decoder were set on the training and validation pages of the seven collections of \gls{rq}~3. No test page took part in this choice, but the target collections did. The results without fine-tuning are therefore not fully independent of the target collections, and a leave-target-out selection would give a stricter estimate. The collections of \gls{rq}~3 differ in script, layout, line density, and degradation. The analyses relate the transfer to the size and the spacing of the lines, but the effect of degradation is not tested separately.
